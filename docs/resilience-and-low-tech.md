# Liberation Mass — Resilience and Low-Tech Continuity

Liberation Mass should remain hostable when the preferred digital tools are unavailable.

That does not mean every gathering needs disaster-response infrastructure.

It means the gathering itself should not become dependent on one website, app, cloud account, projector, QR code, or organizer's phone.

> **Digital tools may coordinate the gathering. They should not be the only place the gathering knows how to exist.**

---

## 1. Minimum viable gathering

If ordinary digital systems fail, a Mass can still happen with:

- a known place;
- people;
- a rough time/window;
- visible access/safety information;
- a way to orient newcomers;
- paper/physical agenda if useful;
- basic supplies;
- people who know the roles;
- clear exits/boundaries;
- food/water/rest where appropriate;
- a way to communicate major changes.

Ritual, mutual aid, conversation, testimony, play, repair, food, and community do not require an API.

---

## 2. Important information should have physical forms

Useful physical/printable artifacts include:

- event address / entrance / access notes;
- run of show;
- role/steward roster;
- emergency contacts;
- consent/access cards;
- room/space map;
- public transit/getting-home notes;
- supply checklist;
- mutual-aid/resource sheet;
- public follow-up contact;
- QR/URL to current digital information when available.

Print should display a **generated/reviewed date** so stale information is visible.

---

## 3. QR codes are enhancements, not doors

A participant should not need a functioning smartphone/data plan to:

- find the entrance;
- know where the restroom is;
- understand the agenda;
- access consent expectations;
- find a quiet space;
- learn how to ask for help;
- participate in a basic mutual-aid process.

QR codes can provide deeper/current information but should not become the only interface.

---

## 4. Power failure

A gathering should consider what changes when power fails.

Depending on venue/time:

- emergency lighting;
- accessible exit implications;
- elevators;
- sound system;
- projectors/screens;
- refrigeration/food safety;
- payment/donation systems;
- device charging;
- ventilation;
- mobility equipment charging;
- public transit conditions.

The answer may simply be:

> gathering cannot safely continue; close clearly.

Resilience is not stubborn continuation at any cost.

---

## 5. Network/application failure

If the event page or Community Infrastructure instance is unavailable:

- physical signage remains authoritative about the actual room;
- stewards communicate changes verbally/physically;
- printed agenda/access notes remain usable with visible timestamp;
- participant lists should not need to be recreated from memory unless genuinely necessary;
- offline/manual participation remains valid;
- later digital records can note what actually happened.

> **The database is not the gathering.**

---

## 6. Low-tech mutual aid

Mutual aid can degrade to:

- bulletin board;
- paper Need/Offer cards;
- steward match board;
- verbal announcements where privacy permits;
- phone/SMS follow-up;
- physical Resource table/free store.

Privacy still matters.

Do not put sensitive Needs on a public wall merely because the digital private workflow is unavailable.

Manual fallback must preserve consent/safety boundaries as far as practical.

---

## 7. Role continuity

If one organizer disappears, the gathering should not become impossible.

Useful practices:

- at least two people understand critical venue access;
- role responsibilities documented simply;
- key contacts available to more than one steward;
- supplies/checklists stored somewhere accessible;
- no single phone/account contains the only copy of the plan;
- local adaptations are documented enough to hand off.

This operationalizes “roles share work, not power.”

---

## 8. Communication hierarchy during disruption

Possible fallback order:

```text
primary Community/Event page
        ↓
email / SMS / group communication
        ↓
public static page / phone contact
        ↓
physical venue signage
        ↓
in-person steward communication
```

Different nodes may use different channels.

The key is that participants can tell **which source is current**.

---

## 9. Space changes are real state

A digital system may say:

> Being Space: Room B

but if a pipe leaks and Being Space moves to the garden room, the actual gathering state has changed.

Hosts should be able to:

- change physical signage immediately;
- announce verbally;
- update digital systems later;
- preserve the reason/context if useful.

The physical layer does not wait for synchronization.

---

## 10. Emergency powers

Safety may require immediate steward action.

But “emergency” should not create an undefined permanent leadership role.

After significant safety action:

- document what happened appropriately;
- preserve confidentiality;
- review role/policy implications;
- retire temporary measures;
- repair affected relationships/processes where possible.

---

## 11. Tabletop exercises

A node can occasionally test:

- website unavailable;
- organizer absent;
- venue changes room;
- elevator fails;
- power outage before event;
- transit disruption;
- contact list unavailable;
- printed agenda is stale;
- phone network overloaded.

Ask:

> What would we actually do—not what does the handbook claim we would do?

---

## 12. After failure

When systems return:

- record important changes;
- update stale access/venue information;
- reconcile mutual-aid commitments made offline;
- replace used emergency supplies;
- update contact/role information;
- note what fallback failed;
- keep the lesson without fetishizing crisis.

---

## Working principle

> **Liberation Mass should lose polish before it loses welcome, care, consent, orientation, or the ability to gather.**
