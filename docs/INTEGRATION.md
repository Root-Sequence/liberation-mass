# Liberation Mass — Integration

**Status:** living practice note  
**Scope:** how Liberation Mass connects its own practices and optional external tools without creating unnecessary centralization or dependency

Liberation Mass combines many kinds of activity:

- gathering;
- ritual;
- testimony;
- mutual aid;
- collective action;
- working groups;
- access and care;
- food and shared resources;
- culture and memory;
- federation among local variants.

Integration does **not** mean forcing all of these into one platform, one workflow, one database, or one authority structure.

> **Integrate what needs to coordinate. Preserve boundaries where boundaries protect people, autonomy, culture, or resilience.**

---

## 1. Practice before tooling

Liberation Mass should remain hostable with ordinary means:

- conversation;
- paper;
- printed access notes;
- email;
- messaging;
- shared documents;
- physical sign-up sheets;
- community memory;
- whatever tools a local node can actually maintain.

A tool is useful when it reduces avoidable burden without becoming a prerequisite for belonging.

No digital integration should become a test of whether a gathering is a “real” Liberation Mass.

---

## 2. Integrate by capability, not by total platform

Different needs may be served by different tools.

Examples:

```text
public invitation       → public web / flyer / calendar
sensitive coordination  → appropriate private channel
working-group planning  → local chosen tools
mutual aid              → voluntary Needs / Offers process
access information      → public before arrival
community memory        → consent-aware local records
federation              → selective sharing between nodes
```

The correct integration question is:

> **What information or action actually needs to cross this boundary?**

not:

> How do we put the entire community into one system?

---

## 3. Public and private information are different layers

Public material may include:

- what Liberation Mass is;
- dates and general locations;
- what to expect;
- accessibility information;
- cost / sliding-scale information;
- whether someone can come alone or observe;
- public contact paths;
- public guides and remixable modules.

Sensitive or contextual material may include:

- participant needs;
- testimony;
- conflict information;
- care requests;
- private working-group discussion;
- operational safety details;
- contact information;
- internal steward notes.

Publishing the first category does not justify exposing the second.

---

## 4. Roles should integrate work without accumulating hidden power

Liberation Mass uses rotating roles rather than fixed leadership.

Integration should therefore preserve:

- scoped responsibilities;
- time-bounded roles;
- shared knowledge rather than private gatekeeping;
- documented handoffs;
- revocability;
- visibility into important decisions where safety permits;
- separation between technical access and social authority.

A person who controls a calendar, server, password manager, mailing list, or registration system may hold **de facto power** even if the social role is described as merely administrative.

Technical integration should make that power legible and reducible rather than pretending it does not exist.

---

## 5. Working groups

Working groups should be able to coordinate independently while remaining appropriately connected to the wider Commons.

Useful shared outputs may include:

- current purpose;
- open requests;
- decisions that affect others;
- upcoming activities;
- resource needs;
- what requires Commons-level discussion.

Not every working-group conversation needs to be globally visible.

See [`working-groups.md`](working-groups.md) and the federation documentation.

---

## 6. Mutual aid and shared resources

Mutual aid should integrate with gathering life without becoming a score, debt ledger, or compulsory volunteering system.

A useful flow may be:

```text
Need / Offer
    ↓
possible match
    ↓
private willingness
    ↓
actual commitment
    ↓
follow-through / resolution
```

A match is not consent. Visibility is not obligation.

Shared resources should make maintenance and stewardship visible rather than treating available objects, spaces, food, vehicles, or tools as frictionless inventory.

---

## 7. Community Infrastructure relationship

The separate [Community Infrastructure](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/community-infrastructure) project may eventually support or interoperate with some Liberation Mass capabilities.

See [`community-infrastructure.md`](community-infrastructure.md) for the detailed relationship.

Potential areas include:

- gathering discovery;
- “what to expect” pages;
- accessibility notes;
- calendars;
- rotating roles;
- working groups;
- Needs & Offers;
- Resource sharing;
- Community Knowledge;
- consent-aware communication;
- federation between autonomous nodes;
- resilient/offline participation.

Boundary:

> **Liberation Mass is a gathering and practice. Community Infrastructure is infrastructure.**

Neither should become the authority that makes the other legitimate.

---

## 8. Federation integration

Local nodes may share selected information without becoming one centrally administered organization.

Possible federation layers include:

- public node discovery;
- shared public events;
- reusable hosting materials;
- working-group collaboration;
- shared campaigns or actions;
- selected Commons summaries;
- seasonal convergence;
- resource or mutual-aid coordination where appropriate.

Federation should preserve the ability of a node to:

- differ;
- refuse participation;
- keep information local;
- fork practices;
- leave;
- continue functioning independently.

See [`federation.md`](federation.md), [`federation-and-working-groups.md`](federation-and-working-groups.md), and related federation guides.

---

## 9. Memory and archives

Liberation Mass may create:

- public zines;
- Commons logs;
- photographs;
- art;
- recordings;
- participant testimony;
- decisions;
- event materials;
- local histories.

These do not all share one consent context.

A public event does not make every participant contribution public. A record useful to local continuity does not automatically belong in a permanent archive.

If material is ever contributed to the autonomous **Museum of Ordinary Life**, that transfer must use the Museum's own submission, consent, provenance, and stewardship process.

---

## 10. Graceful degradation

Integrated systems should have understandable fallback paths.

Ask:

- What happens if the website is down?
- What happens if a steward loses access?
- Can people still find the gathering?
- Can access notes still be distributed?
- Can mutual aid continue?
- Can a local node function without the wider federation?
- Can people participate without installing an app?

The goal is not technical purity.

It is continuity of human capacity.

---

## Integration test

Before adding an integration, ask:

```text
WHAT HUMAN NEED DOES THIS SUPPORT?

WHAT INFORMATION MUST CROSS A BOUNDARY?

WHO GAINS ACCESS OR POWER?

WHO MUST MAINTAIN IT?

CAN SOMEONE PARTICIPATE WITHOUT IT?

CAN A LOCAL NODE REFUSE IT?

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT FAILS?

WHAT SHOULD REMAIN PRIVATE OR LOCAL?

HOW CAN PEOPLE EXIT?
```

If the integration creates more compulsory infrastructure than community capacity, simplify it.

---

> **The gathering is the point. The infrastructure should remain in service to it.**
