# Liberation Mass ↔ Community Infrastructure

**Status:** Relationship note / exploratory

Liberation Mass and Community Infrastructure are independent projects with overlapping questions.

Liberation Mass is primarily an **embodied gathering, commons, ritual, mutual-aid, and collective-practice project**.

Community Infrastructure is primarily a **digital/community coordination infrastructure project**.

Neither should become a dependency of the other.

The relationship is useful because Liberation Mass creates real social situations that digital infrastructure often abstracts away: arrival, uncertainty, access needs, rotating responsibility, mutual aid, consent, aftercare, conflict, belonging, shared memory, and the difference between holding a role and holding power.

Community Infrastructure can in turn test whether digital tools can support those situations without replacing the gathering itself.

---

## Shared questions

### Stewardship vs. authority

Liberation Mass uses the principle:

> **Roles share work, not power.**

Community Infrastructure asks the software version:

- What permissions does a steward actually receive?
- How long do they last?
- Can they be delegated or revoked?
- Who sees what they did?
- Does infrastructure administration silently create greater power than the social role intends?

A digital system can therefore expose where an anti-hierarchical social principle needs more operational detail.

### Consent and exit

Liberation Mass centers opt-in participation, consent, and the ability to leave.

Community Infrastructure can test corresponding digital patterns:

- optional participation rather than mandatory membership;
- progressive disclosure;
- clear audience controls;
- easy withdrawal of Offers or commitments;
- temporary event participation;
- leaving Groups/Communities without unnecessary penalties;
- distinct consent for publication, archival preservation, or secondary reuse.

### Accessibility

Liberation Mass calls for access notes, quiet spaces, seating, captions/printouts, and multiple ways to participate.

Community Infrastructure extends that into digital participation:

- public access information before arrival;
- keyboard/screen-reader/zoom support;
- plain-language flows;
- low-bandwidth and old-device support;
- print, email, SMS, and kiosk paths;
- “what to expect” information for newcomers;
- participation without installing an app where possible.

### Mutual aid

Liberation Mass includes a Mutual Aid Space and resource-sharing practices.

Community Infrastructure's Needs ↔ Offers and Resource Commons work may eventually support:

- pre-event Needs and Offers;
- rides or accessibility support;
- shared supplies;
- food/resource coordination;
- volunteer needs;
- post-event follow-up;
- local mutual-aid networks that persist between gatherings.

This should remain voluntary and avoid reputation scoring or obligation pressure.

### Discovery and belonging

Liberation Mass is a useful case for Community Discovery because a potential participant may need more than a title and date.

A humane Discovery Profile could answer:

- What actually happens there?
- Can I come alone?
- Can I observe without participating?
- Is it beginner-friendly?
- What does it cost?
- What access accommodations exist?
- What should I bring?
- Can I leave early?
- Is there a contact person?
- Which parts are public, private, or sensitive?

Community Infrastructure should optimize for **a safe, legible first step**, not attendance growth or conversion.

## Possible future Community Infrastructure support

If useful to real Liberation Mass nodes, Community Infrastructure could eventually provide or interoperate with:

- node / gathering discovery;
- public “what to expect” and accessibility pages;
- event/calendar export;
- rotating role coordination;
- working groups;
- scoped announcements and Community Home-style catch-up;
- Needs & Offers;
- shared Resource inventories;
- volunteer coordination;
- consent-aware communication;
- public vs. private operational information;
- Community Knowledge / host guides;
- local memory and post-event learnings;
- federation between autonomous nodes;
- resilient/offline communication paths.

These are possibilities, not requirements.

Liberation Mass should still be hostable with ordinary low-tech tools, paper, conversation, email, or other systems. A gathering should never require Community Infrastructure in order to be legitimate.

---

## Potential pilot value

A future Liberation Mass node could be a useful Community Infrastructure pilot context because it combines:

- public discovery;
- newcomer uncertainty;
- temporary participation;
- recurring gatherings;
- rotating stewardship;
- working groups;
- mutual aid;
- accessibility needs;
- public and sensitive information;
- community memory;
- federation between local variants.

That combination would stress many parts of the model at once.

It may also be **too complex for the very first pilot**, which is currently intended to use lower-risk, narrower workflows. Pilot selection should follow actual organizer/participant needs rather than ecosystem neatness.

---

## What Community Infrastructure can learn from Liberation Mass

The largest contribution may not be a feature list.

Liberation Mass insists that communities contain things software tends to erase:

- ritual;
- grief;
- joy;
- silence;
- sensory needs;
- testimony;
- embodied presence;
- cultural meaning;
- informal care;
- the freedom not to optimize everything.

That is a useful constraint on Community Infrastructure.

A technically efficient Community platform that supports tasks but flattens community into administration would be incomplete.

---

## What Liberation Mass can learn from Community Infrastructure

Digital implementation can expose social questions that aspirational language leaves unresolved:

- When exactly does a steward have authority?
- Who can remove someone from a space or Group?
- How is that decision recorded and challenged?
- What information must remain private?
- How are temporary roles rotated in practice?
- How does federation preserve local autonomy?
- Who maintains the infrastructure?
- What happens when a node loses access or connectivity?

The goal is not to make Liberation Mass more bureaucratic. It is to let practical friction reveal where principles need more precision.

---

## Boundary

> **Liberation Mass is a gathering/practice. Community Infrastructure is infrastructure.**

Software should support human community life without becoming the thing community life is organized around.
