# Liberation Mass — Idea Trails

**Status:** Local relationship index  
**Canonical cross-project map:** [`Root-Sequence/root-sequence/IDEA_TRAILS.md`](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/blob/main/IDEA_TRAILS.md)

Liberation Mass contributes an **embodied-practice perspective** to several Root Sequence Idea Trails. It asks what principles look like when people actually gather in rooms, parks, homes, halls, kitchens, dance floors, quiet spaces, working groups, and mutual-aid contexts.

It should not become a software test harness or theoretical case study at the expense of being a real gathering practice.

---

## Discoverability & Belonging

Liberation Mass asks:

> What does a newcomer need to know to feel able to show up?

Relevant practices:

- access notes;
- clear description of Commons vs. Rite;
- whether someone can come alone;
- ability to observe or participate partially;
- cost/sliding-scale information;
- public vs. sensitive spaces;
- hosts/stewards who can answer questions;
- newcomer pathways that do not demand immediate identity or commitment.

This trail connects directly to Community Infrastructure Community Discovery.

---

## Stewardship & Authority

Liberation Mass's strongest contribution is:

> **Roles share work, not power.**

Idea Trail questions:

- Do rotating roles actually prevent durable informal hierarchy?
- Which responsibilities need explicit decision authority?
- What happens during safety emergencies?
- How does experience remain useful without turning experienced people into permanent leaders?
- How do local nodes remain autonomous while sharing practices?

Community Infrastructure can expose digital permission questions that make these tensions more concrete.

---

## Mutual Aid, Reciprocity & Need

Liberation Mass integrates mutual aid into community life rather than treating it as a separate charity function.

Relevant practices:

- Mutual Aid Space;
- shared food;
- free store/resource exchange;
- rides/access support;
- skillshares;
- repair/mending;
- post-gathering care and follow-up.

Community Infrastructure Needs ↔ Offers and Resource Commons may eventually support these practices without turning them into quantified generosity or debt.

---

## Accessibility & Participation

Liberation Mass provides embodied tests for whether access language survives real events.

Relevant practices:

- quiet/Being Spaces;
- seating;
- captions/printouts;
- clear sensory information;
- multiple levels of participation;
- ability to leave;
- aftercare;
- Kids & Families Space;
- physical access notes;
- consent signals.

Community Infrastructure can help publish and coordinate this information but should not reduce accessibility to metadata alone.

## Federation & Autonomy

Liberation Mass already contains node/federation thinking.

Idea Trail questions:

- What is shared across nodes?
- What remains local?
- How do local variants diverge without becoming “incorrect” branches?
- How do shared decisions avoid centralizing the project?
- How do nodes recognize or recommend one another?

This connects to Community Infrastructure Community-to-Community discovery and federation research.

---

## Maintenance & Invisible Labor

Gatherings make maintenance visible:

- setup;
- supplies;
- access preparation;
- food;
- cleanup;
- facilitation;
- conflict response;
- aftercare;
- documentation;
- outreach;
- role rotation.

A Mass that appears spontaneous may rely on significant invisible labor.

This is useful feedback to Root Sequence, Community Infrastructure, and Coherent World whenever they describe community systems too cleanly.

---

## Growth & the Right to Stay Small

Liberation Mass should be able to exist as:

- one living-room gathering;
- a local recurring Mass;
- several loosely connected nodes;
- a temporary experiment;
- a larger federated practice.

None is inherently more successful because it is larger.

This connects strongly to Community Infrastructure's principle that Communities may remain tiny, private, informal, or non-growing.

---

## Accountability & Consequences

Liberation Mass contributes concrete practices around:

- consent;
- conflict resolution;
- boundaries;
- safety stewardship;
- aftercare;
- sensitive information;
- repair after harm.

Being Human(e) provides broader relational framing; Community Infrastructure explores corresponding digital mechanisms; Root Sequence carries the larger systems/accountability analysis.

---

## Boundary

Liberation Mass is where these trails become **embodied social practice**.

If an Idea Trail makes the gathering more abstract, managerial, or technology-dependent rather than more attentive to actual people in the room, it is being applied badly.
